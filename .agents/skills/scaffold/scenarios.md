# Scenarios

Scenarios belong only to a film. Mirror the reviewed storyline's ordered files, slugs, H2 sequences, H3 scenes, and H4 beats in `docs/scenarios`.

A scenario is a production-capable initial script, not a causal summary and not a final shooting script. It fixes the physical or formal progression, reaction, movement, transition, consequential exchange, and exit state deeply enough that the screenplay does not invent essential scene mechanics.

For every H4, enact the exact temporal and spatial conditions at the precision this layer owns. Record entry state, participants or active forces, observation range, immediate objective or organizing process, obstruction where applicable, ordered action and reaction, spatial or informational movement, decisive exchange, knowledge change, and exit state. Write consequential speech, refusal, interruption, or silence with its surrounding action; summarize only incidental exchange.

Before drafting, copy the reviewed storyline unit map and verify one inherited identity per scenario unit. Apply the narrative addressability and scenario staging-block principles. If executable detail reveals a hidden scene or defective boundary, repair the storyline first and propagate the corrected identity.

## Gate

Start at `scenarios: "draft"` only after storylines are in `review`. Before `evidence`, enact every H4 in order and test physical possibility, timing, resources, settings capabilities, entry and exit continuity, proportional expansion beyond its treatment parent, and every consequential action, exchange, knowledge change, or silence. A summary that merely says these occur is not a scenario.

Every H2/H3/H4 will answer the common unit obligations, cite one exact same-level storyline parent, and cite only settings it uses. Follow [Evidence staging](evidence-staging.md). A discovered settings or storyline defect is repaired at that earlier owner before scenario work resumes.
