# Instance upstream revision

What deriving and placing a population proves wrong in settings or consumed map, model, space, and material units, and what is repaired there. Every selected instance H2 answers for its actual parents.

## Parent revision from instance work {#parent-revision-from-instance-work}

Population work tests prototypes and placement interfaces under repetition, variation, density, contact, and boundary conditions. Report the exact parent defect it exposes: a prototype origin that cannot remain grounded, an extent that causes overlap, a map or space boundary that cannot contain the declared count, a material variation outside the authorized domain, or a settings quantity with no deterministic derivation. Repair the earliest owner; do not hide the defect in per-member transforms or undocumented overrides.

`@evidence` names every upstream target repaired and the population case that exposed it. When this unit exposes no defect, `@evidenceExclude` names the prototypes, boundaries, contacts, variation domains, and quantity basis examined and why they were sufficient. A parentless unit or one population-wide stock sentence covers nothing.

Review question: what did deriving or placing this instance unit prove wrong in its actual parents, or which concrete parent decisions did it test and find sufficient?

Sources: [OpenUSD PointInstancer on prototypes, ids, transforms, and computed extents](https://openusd.org/release/api/class_usd_geom_point_instancer.html); [NASA on tracing test findings to upstream owners](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)
