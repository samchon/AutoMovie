# Research principles

These principles govern the optional research ledger. Research records external support and its production consequence; it does not become a second settings or design catalogue.

## Source identity {#source-identity}

Every research entry identifies the source, the portion used, its publication or retrieval context when that context affects reliability, and whether the source is primary, official, scholarly, or interpretive.

Review question: can another author find the same evidence and tell what kind of authority it carries?

Sources: [W3C Data on the Web Best Practices on data provenance](https://www.w3.org/TR/dwbp/#dataProvenance); [Chicago Manual of Style citation quick guide](https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/tools_citationguide.html)

## Production consequence {#production-consequence}

Every research entry states the exact settings, design, narrative, brief, source, or review decision it supports or constrains. Interesting context without a production consequence remains outside the committed ledger.

Review question: which owned decision changes if this evidence is disproved?

Sources: [W3C PROV-O on entities, activities, and agents in provenance](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/); [NASA systems engineering handbook on traceability from stakeholder expectations to technical requirements](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)

## Uncertainty boundary {#uncertainty-boundary}

Every research entry records material disagreement, missing coverage, or interpretive limits before downstream documents select a production answer. The ledger does not resolve uncertainty by silently averaging incompatible sources.

Review question: what remains unknown or contested after reading this entry, and where is the production choice recorded?

Sources: [NIST Technical Note 1297 on expressing measurement uncertainty](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297); [W3C Data Quality Vocabulary](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dqv/)
