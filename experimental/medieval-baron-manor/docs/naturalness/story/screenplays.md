# Screenplay naturalness principles

Film-specific language questions for final screenplay units. Construction owns what the scene means, looks like, and physically requires; this family owns how frozen dialogue, narration, and audience-read language sound and perform. It does not naturalize external description or blocking.

## Scene pressure shapes expression {#naturalness-scene-pressure}

Dialogue, narration, and audience-read captions take their length, syntax, density, and cadence from the current physical and dramatic pressure. Unlike scenes do not converge on one explanatory frame or interchangeable language rhythm merely because each sentence is locally grammatical. A refrain, ritual, procedural voice, or declared formal system remains when the production owns the recurrence.

Revise only the language realization of already authored speech acts, claims, relations, and audience-readable facts. Copy action lines and mechanically exact appearance, geometry, material, placement, contact, motion, state, sound-event, transition-event, and timing descriptions unchanged. A missing escalation, response, consequence, cue, or timing boundary is a construction defect and cannot be fabricated to improve flow.

Review question: where does this unit's expression respond to its changing pressure, and which passage keeps an imported cadence after the scene has moved on?

Sources: [Zwaan, Langston, and Graesser on situation changes](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x); [Purdue OWL on sentence-pattern variation](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/general_writing/academic_writing/sentence_variety/similar_sentence_patterns_or_rhythms.html)

Read each dialogue, narration, and audience-read text passage in sequence with the unchanged action around it and identify what changes hands: information, leverage, obligation, refusal, attention, mood, bodily response, or audience interpretation. A line fails when it merely restates an adjacent block, announces the scene's meaning as a portable proposition, or shares one interchangeable figure with every speaker or narrator while producing no distinct effect. Repetition that is itself a ritual response, command, confirmation, refrain, or declared voice pattern remains valid when its consequence is legible.

Reword eligible language to reveal the inherited tactic, relation, speaker or narrator identity, or effect. Preserve what every speaker knows, seeks, says, withholds, gains, and loses. Sound-event descriptions and all physical clauses remain exact construction text. If the exchange or narration lacks the substantive content needed to act, return it to scripts or screenplay construction.

Review question: what changes for the hearer, speaker, narrator, scene, or audience after each consequential line, and which line merely duplicates what is already present?

Sources: [Cambridge Companion to Narrative on dialogue as action](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-companion-to-narrative/dialogue/DA260BBCE7DBBA9BCB4196CE7F35ABDC); [Center for Fiction on dialogue function and subtext](https://centerforfiction.org/writing-tools/tips-for-writing-dialogue/)
