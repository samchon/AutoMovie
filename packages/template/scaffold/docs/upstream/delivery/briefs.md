# Brief upstream revision

What bounding a direct delivery and its observations proves wrong in settings or selected design units, and what is repaired there. Every selected brief H2, H3, and H4 answers for its actual parents.

## Parent revision from brief work {#parent-revision-from-brief-work}

Brief work tests whether a direct, non-narrative delivery can be performed and falsified without an unowned creative decision. Report the exact parent defect it exposes: an observation with no threshold, a view that cannot show the promised property, an action outside the subject's capability, a design interface that cannot produce the requested state, or a supposedly bounded result that actually requires narrative causality. Repair settings or the responsible design owner; when independent narrative refinement is required, change the production kind rather than smuggling it into the brief.

`@evidence` names every upstream target repaired and the delivery or observation test that exposed it. When this unit exposes no defect, `@evidenceExclude` names the concrete promise, subject, design interfaces, observation condition, and threshold examined and why they were sufficient. A parentless unit or generic readiness claim covers nothing.

Review question: what did bounding this brief unit prove wrong in its actual settings or design parents, or which concrete parent decisions did it test and find sufficient?

Sources: [NASA on verification objectives, methods, and traceability](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/system-engineering-handbook-appendix/); [W3C PROV-O on derivation and attributed responsibility](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)
