# Direct brief obligations

Duties the complete brief H2 owner population satisfies across its bounded delivery, shot, and observation hierarchy. An item appears once or more, as its wording requires, across those owners; H3 and H4 descendants provide the owned detail rather than repeating the obligation population. They permit no exclusion.

## Single-scope eligibility {#single-scope-eligibility}

The complete brief population states one observable production question that can be completely answered without a multi-scene causal, choice, or disclosure structure. If downstream work would have to invent such a structure, the production is a film regardless of runtime.

Review question: can the requested result be stated as one bounded falsifiable question, or does it require independent deliveries or a causal ladder?

This obligation decides whether the requested result qualifies for the brief shape. The no-narrative-smuggling principle separately checks every authored unit after selection against that boundary.

Sources: [NIST definition of verification as objective evidence against specified requirements](https://www.nist.gov/glossary-term/34441); [NASA systems engineering handbook on defining verification objectives](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)

## Brief unit addressability {#brief-unit-addressability}

The complete brief population gives every independently changeable, citable, or reviewable delivery, shot, and observation its own H2, H3, or H4 address. An H3 does not hide a second shot with its own composition, viewing condition, or acceptance boundary in direct prose, and an H4 does not combine observations that use different conditions or falsify different claims. Split hidden independent work and merge fragments that cannot move, change, or be judged independently.

A change of camera, time, subject, pass, or viewing condition is evidence of a possible boundary rather than an automatic split. H2 and H3 units organize their actual descendants instead of substituting direct prose for the address that an independently useful descendant requires.

Review question: does the brief population hide any independently changeable delivery, shot, or observation, or does any split separate material that cannot move, change, be cited, or be judged independently?

This obligation owns the semantic boundaries of the brief population. Single-scope eligibility decides whether an independent delivery means the production is not a brief; addressability refuses to hide that delivery merely to preserve the selected shape. Observable progression owns whether the requested observation contract is completely allocated across the resulting population, and the brief information-structure principle owns the arrangement inside each resulting unit.

Sources: [Zacks and colleagues on event segmentation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8710938/); [DOME on dynamic hierarchical outlining](https://aclanthology.org/2025.naacl-long.63/); [NASA on unique identification and one requirement per statement](https://swehb.nasa.gov/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=146540037)

## Observable progression {#observable-progression}

The complete brief population allocates its observable contract: the H2 delivery states the bounded question and overall initial-to-terminal progression, each H3 shot assigns the portion it presents, and each H4 observation states the viewing condition and result that can answer or falsify that portion. The units cite the settings and every applicable model, space, material, instance, motion, and system design they use rather than restating them.

Review question: what exact observation proves the complete brief progression occurred as requested?
