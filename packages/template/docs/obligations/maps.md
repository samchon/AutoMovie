# Map obligations

These roles are distributed across the map-design H2 population. Map owns the broad world's content, coordinates, networks, and state; spaces consumes the adopted site boundary and access interface and owns the site and building topology within it.

## Addressable map decisions {#addressable-map-decisions}

The map population gives every independently citable extent, coordinate basis, feature family, network, phase, source adoption, site interface, derived representation, and world observation one stable H2 owner.

Review question: which world decision could change independently and is still hidden inside another owner's prose, drawing, dataset, or implementation?

This item owns the map population's complete H2 decomposition. The addressable-world principle makes each resulting H2 preserve the identities it states or consumes.

Sources: [NASA on unique, bidirectionally traceable requirements](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/system-engineering-handbook-appendix/); [OGC CityGML 3.0 on modular thematic ownership](https://docs.ogc.org/is/21-006r2/21-006r2.html)

## World extent and site interface {#map-world-site-interface}

The population establishes the canonical world frame, units, declared extent, map feature identities, and every site boundary and access node consumed by a space branch. Map owns the parcel or site boundary as a world feature and the external terrain, water, and transport network through the named access node. Spaces owns the topology, envelope, openings, dimensions, and routes inside that boundary. Settlement-scale building footprints may locate buildings in the world, but the building envelope and interior remain space owners.

Review question: can a site, building, subject, camera, vehicle, or system enter the world at one cited frame and interface without map and spaces authoring the same boundary, route, or building fact twice?

Sources: [OGC Topic 2 on coordinate reference systems and transformations](https://docs.ogc.org/as/18-005r5/18-005r5.html); [buildingSMART on explicit space-boundary relationships](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4_3/HTML/lexical/IfcRelSpaceBoundary.htm)

## Selected world content and relations {#map-world-content-relations}

Across its H2 owners, the population accounts for every world family this production actually uses: terrain and ground, inland or coastal water, vegetation and ecology, parcels and land use, settlements and public space, roads and other transport, crossings and infrastructure, weather and seasonal conditions, temporal change, and externally adopted spatial data or placed assets. Each selected family states its contacts, dependencies, source and confidence, current state, and consequences for other selected families. An unused family is not invented merely to fill a catalogue.

Review question: which selected world feature has no owner for its relation to the terrain, networks, site, population, time state, or source from which its behavior and placement follow?

Sources: [OGC CityGML 3.0 on terrain, water, vegetation, transportation, land use, city objects, and versioning in one model](https://docs.ogc.org/is/21-006r2/21-006r2.html); [NASA systems engineering handbook on interfaces and allocated responsibility](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)

## World temporal state and alternatives {#map-world-temporal-state}

The population defines the canonical current phase or interval, state transitions the delivery actually uses, and the selection and comparison rule for alternatives. A static work states its fixed state once; it does not manufacture change, while a time-varying work traces every selected feature and network consequence across the affected interval.

Review question: which feature, relation, route, or observation could change with time or alternative selection without the population identifying its valid state and affected outputs?

Sources: [OGC CityGML 3.0 on versions and version transitions](https://docs.ogc.org/is/21-006r2/21-006r2.html); [Web Animations on explicit time values and sampled state](https://www.w3.org/TR/web-animations-1/)

## World scale, partition, and population {#map-world-scale-partition}

The population defines the scale and level-of-detail policy, partition or tile boundaries, deterministic population basis, and identity-preservation rule applicable to the selected map content. An untiled or uninstanced work states that fixed boundary once; it does not manufacture streaming or population work.

Review question: which feature, boundary, route, quantity, or identity could change across scale, detail, tile order, seam, or population evaluation without an explicit preservation or refusal rule?

Sources: [OGC 3D Tiles on hierarchical spatial content and levels of detail](https://www.ogc.org/standard/3dtiles/); [OpenUSD PointInstancer on stable ids, prototype indices, transforms, and extents](https://openusd.org/release/api/class_usd_geom_point_instancer.html)

## World source resolution and staleness {#map-world-source-resolution}

The population defines how adopted and authored inputs become the resolved world, which source identity, coverage, accuracy, uncertainty, transform, and conversion loss survive that derivation, and which dependent maps, routes, quantities, renders, and deliveries become stale when an input or rule changes.

Review question: which resolved world fact cannot be traced to its current input and derivation, or which changed input leaves a dependent result looking current?

Sources: [W3C PROV-O on derivation and invalidation](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/); [W3C Data Quality Vocabulary on quality measurements and provenance](https://www.w3.org/TR/vocab-dqv/)

## Map review set {#map-review-set}

The population defines a finite set of plan or map views, sections, elevations or profiles, neutral perspectives, traversals, network and quantity checks, state comparisons, source-resolution comparisons, and boundary or seam observations sufficient to falsify every selected world family and its site interfaces independently of dramatic shot composition.

Review question: which finite observations expose every critical terrain, water, feature, route, population, phase, coordinate, and site-contact claim at the scale where it is promised?

Sources: [NASA on definitive requirement verification matrices](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/system-engineering-handbook-appendix/); [OGC CityGML 3.0 Abstract Test Suite](https://docs.ogc.org/is/21-006r2/21-006r2.html#toc45)
