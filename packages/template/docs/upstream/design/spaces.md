# Space upstream revision

What laying out a site, building, room, or route proves wrong in settings and any map units it consumes, and what is repaired there. Every selected space H2 answers for its actual parents.

## Settings and map revision from space work {#settings-and-map-revision-from-space-work}

Space work turns access, capacity, circulation, and site facts into topology and clear dimensions. Report the exact parent defect it exposes: a user or load profile too vague to size a route, incompatible coordinate or clearance rules, a map boundary or access node that cannot meet the required circulation, or a capability and acceptance condition that leaves no viable layout. Repair the settings or map owner before revising the space; do not conceal the gap with a convenient local dimension, doorway, or route.

`@evidence` names every upstream target repaired and the spatial test that exposed it. When this unit exposes no defect, `@evidenceExclude` names the concrete profiles, constraints, site interfaces, and observations examined and why they supported this topology. A parentless unit or generic sufficiency statement covers nothing.

Review question: what did laying out this space unit prove wrong in its actual settings or map parents, or which concrete parent decisions did it test and find sufficient?

Sources: [buildingSMART on explicit space boundaries and their relating elements](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4_3/HTML/lexical/IfcRelSpaceBoundary.htm); [NASA on interface definition and upstream corrective action](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)
