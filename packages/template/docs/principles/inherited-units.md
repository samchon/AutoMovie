# Inherited-unit principles

This checklist binds every selected map, model, space, material, instance, motion, system, and brief H2/H3/H4. It tests the work each derived unit contributes beyond its actual settings and design parents; settings and research do not select it because they inherit no authored production unit.

## Derived parent differentiation {#derived-parent-differentiation}

Every derived unit adds a decision owned by its declared layer beyond the actual parent units it consumes. A map resolves settings into world extent, features, networks, and state; a space resolves settings and map interfaces into topology, dimensions, and routes; a model resolves settings and space interfaces into bounded construction and writable representation; materials, instances, motions, and systems add their own binding, population, transition, and process decisions over cited design owners; a brief turns settings and selected designs into a bounded delivery and falsifiable observation hierarchy.

Reformatting a parent, copying its fields into a table, expanding its prose without a layer-owned decision, or adding an implementation convenience is not differentiation. The child names its actual parents and the local decision they authorize but do not already contain. If it has no parent, fix lineage rather than fabricate a comparison. If its parents leave no legitimate decision for this layer, repair the earliest owning parent or remove the redundant child.

Review question: what decision owned by this derived unit's declared layer does it add beyond every actual parent it consumes?

This item asks what one complete-looking child adds to its parents. `principles/common.md#substantive-completion` asks whether it is a complete artifact of its own layer at all, while `obligations/common.md#layer-boundary` routes each decision to the specialist population entitled to own it. The population's domain file under `docs/upstream/{design,story,delivery}` separately records what authoring the child proved wrong in those parents and permits a concrete negative when they were sufficient.

Sources: [NASA on bidirectional traceability and allocated design responsibility](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/); [W3C PROV-O on derivation, attribution, and responsibility](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)
