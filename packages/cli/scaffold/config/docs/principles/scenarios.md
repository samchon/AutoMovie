# Scenario principles

Scenarios translate storyline units into physically executable scene progression. They decide staging state and action order but do not write the final audiovisual screenplay or implementation.

## Entry and exit state {#entry-exit-state}

Every scenario unit states the participating subjects, place, relevant knowledge, and physical state at entry and the observable state left at exit. It inherits the storyline's change and makes that change stageable.

Review question: can the unit start from its predecessor and hand a complete changed state to its successor?

Sources: [Zacks and colleagues on event boundaries](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8710938/); [Oregon State University on setting as time and place](https://liberalarts.oregonstate.edu/wlf/what-setting)

## Executable progression {#executable-progression}

Every scenario unit orders concrete actions and reactions that respect settings capabilities and constraints. It does not choose a representation interface or interpolation path, and it does not solve missing production facts by inventing them inside staging.

Review question: could every stated action be assigned to an existing subject capability without another design decision?

Sources: [Zwaan, Langston, and Graesser on spatial, temporal, causal, protagonist, and goal continuity](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x); [Oregon State University on interaction between setting and action](https://liberalarts.oregonstate.edu/wlf/what-setting)

## Scenario boundary {#scenario-boundary}

Every scenario unit specifies physical progression and its dramatic legibility while leaving final wording, camera grammar, editing, exact shot duration, and source-level interpolation to the script, shot, and motion contracts that own them.

Review question: which sentence would have to move downward because it chooses presentation rather than physical progression?

Sources: [Plan-and-Write](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33017378); [NASA systems engineering handbook on allocation and traceability](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/)
