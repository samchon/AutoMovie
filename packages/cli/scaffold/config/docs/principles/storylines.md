# Storyline principles

Storylines state what changes, why it changes, and what the audience understands. They remain independent of executable blocking, exact timing, camera, and implementation.

## Causal turn {#causal-turn}

Every storyline unit states the initiating condition, a consequential action or choice, and the changed condition that follows. Coincidence may initiate a turn but does not replace the causal relation the unit promises to dramatize.

Review question: what action or choice produces the unit's irreversible difference?

Sources: [Zwaan, Langston, and Graesser on goal and causation continuity](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1995.tb00513.x); [Zacks and colleagues on event boundaries](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8710938/)

## Audience change {#audience-change}

Every storyline unit names what the audience expects, learns, reinterprets, or withholds across the turn. This H2 owns the before-and-after information change inside one unit; `obligations/storylines.md#audience-route` separately owns the production-wide order in which those unit changes are delivered. Neither is a camera angle or line-by-line disclosure plan.

Review question: how is the audience's understanding different at the unit's end?

Sources: [Brewer and Lichtenstein on story structure and affect](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X00017568); [Living Handbook of Narratology on narratorial representation](https://www-archiv.fdm.uni-hamburg.de/lhn/node/44.html)

## Treatment boundary {#treatment-boundary}

Every storyline unit is specific about characters, stakes, order, and consequence while leaving spatial execution, second-by-second timing, dialogue wording, and shot construction to lower layers.

Review question: does the unit contain enough dramatic causality for a scenario to stage without already behaving like a scenario or screenplay?

Sources: [Plan-and-Write](https://doi.org/10.1609/aaai.v33i01.33017378); [Detailed Outline Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10077)
