# Screenplay principles

Screenplay files are the final human-readable audiovisual contract for film shots and their audience-facing edit tracks. They preserve their scenario and storyline ancestry while choosing exactly what the audience sees, reads, and hears.

## Filmable expression {#filmable-expression}

Every script unit expresses present-tense visible action, audience-facing text, audible speech or sound, and only the contextual information needed to interpret them. Internal intent appears through an observable cue or remains owned by the storyline rather than becoming an unfilmable instruction.

Review question: what would the audience see, read, or hear for every sentence in the unit?

Sources: [Academy Nicholl Fellowships screenplay resources](https://www.oscars.org/nicholl); [BBC Writersroom script library and format examples](https://www.bbc.co.uk/writersroom/scripts/)

## Timing allocation {#timing-allocation}

Every time-critical script action or hold states an exact duration, boundary, or explicit shot-owned timing reference at the narrowest unit that owns it. Qualitative pacing may explain effect but cannot replace time needed by deterministic rendering. Stable H2, H3, and H4 identity is governed separately by the narrative-unit principle and the exact lineage graph.

Review question: can a shot recover every render-critical time without translating an adverb or inventing a number?

Sources: [Web Animations timing model](https://www.w3.org/TR/web-animations-1/#timing-model); [SMPTE standards overview for time and synchronization](https://www.smpte.org/standards)

## Realization-ready contract {#realization-ready-contract}

Every script scene contains the complete dramatic and audiovisual requirement its local shot and audience-facing edit tracks must jointly satisfy, while leaving engine calls, geometry construction, interpolation, and editorial mapping to their distinct source contracts. A source may realize its assigned portion but may not invent missing story action, caption content, or sound.

Review question: could independent shot and edit implementers build one equivalent blocking pass from this scene without making different narrative or audience-content decisions?

Sources: [NASA systems engineering handbook on verification-ready requirements](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/systems-engineering-handbook/); [Academy Digital Source Master project](https://www.oscars.org/science-technology/sci-tech-projects/academy-digital-source-master)
