# Form and silhouette

Rules a modelled subject answers about its shape. This production renders a
blocking pass, so a subject is judged on what its contour carries rather than
on its surface: what survives distance, compression and a small raster is the
outline.

## The pose is judged from the contour alone {#silhouette-test}

Judge a pose from the `mask` or `outline` pass before the `beauty` pass. The
oldest staging test is a black shape on white with no texture and no detail: if
the contour does not carry the action, no material will rescue it, and a beauty
frame flatters exactly the confusions the silhouette exposes — limbs
overlapping the torso, two subjects merging into one mass, a gesture pointing
into the body.

The passes exist for this. A shot that declares only `beauty` review frames has
declared that nobody will look at its shape.

Sources: `CAPTURE_FRAME` on passes; `REVIEW_SHOT` on staging readability.

## One line of action, and negative space around it {#line-of-action}

A staged figure reads because one dominant line runs through it and because the
space around that line is open. Two competing lines read as neither, and a limb
crossing the torso closes the shape that made the gesture visible.

State which line the pose turns on. A pose whose intent cannot be named in one
clause is a pose that will be described differently by every reviewer.

## Detail is spent at the distance it survives {#detail-at-distance}

Resolve a form only as finely as the widest shot that shows it. Detail below
that threshold is cost with no effect on any frame, and a shape tuned in close-
up can vanish in a crowd while a shape tuned for a crowd is crude in close-up.

Say which distance a subject was tuned for, so a later shot that violates it is
a decision rather than a surprise.

Sources: `principles/authoring/settings.md#reads-at-review-distance`;
`CINEMATOGRAPHY` on subject scale against camera distance.

## A proxy is honest about being a proxy {#proxy-is-declared}

A primitive standing in for a subject is a legitimate step. What it may not do
is pretend: a cuboid that was meant to become a chair is recorded as unmet
work, not counted as a chair, and never dressed to look finished from one
angle.

Sources: `principles/authoring/settings.md#no-placeholder-final`.
