# Motion and timing

Rules authored movement answers. This production's output is a blocking pass,
so motion is judged on whether the change is legible and whether the claimed
contact actually happened — not on polish.

## Time is stated in seconds {#seconds-not-adverbs}

Every duration a production depends on is a number in seconds, in the record
the engine reads. "Briefly" and "a beat" are not durations, and the engine
cannot read them. Where prose gives a time, it must be a time the contract
carries.

Sources: `principles/source/shots.md#timing-comes-from-the-contract`;
`EDITING`.

## Measure the contact you claimed {#measure-the-contact}

A foot that plants, a hand that grips, a leaf that meets its frame: if the
motion claims a contact, measure it rather than describing it. "The feet stay
planted" and "the ankle stays within 2 mm of the ground function across the
interval" are different claims, and only the second can fail.

Sources: `MOTION` on measuring contact; `principles/common.md#stated-basis`.

## A change reads because something did not change {#change-needs-a-still}

Movement is legible against stillness. When everything in the frame moves, the
audience reads none of it, so a staged change names what holds still while it
happens — the rows that keep their order, the ground that does not drift, the
camera that stays put.

## Screen direction is established once and kept {#screen-direction}

Once a shot establishes which way a subject faces and which side of the action
the camera sits on, later shots keep that side unless a move is authored to
cross it. Crossing the line without authoring the crossing swaps left and right
between two cuts, and the audience reads it as two different places.

State the side the sequence is shot from, so a later shot that crosses is a
decision somebody made rather than a camera somebody moved.

Sources: `CINEMATOGRAPHY`; `EDITING` on continuity across a cut.

## A pose is held long enough to be read {#holds-are-authored}

A pose the audience must read is held, and the hold is a stated duration rather
than whatever the interpolation leaves. A gesture that arrives and immediately
leaves reads as noise at delivery raster, and the frames that would have proven
it were never long enough to sample.

Sources: `EDITING` on how a cut reads against duration.
