# Scale and proportion

Rules a subject answers about size. The two words are not synonyms and the
confusion is expensive: **scale** is the relation between a thing and a
reference outside it — a standing figure, the ground, the frame — and
**proportion** is the relation among the parts inside it. A model can be
perfectly proportioned and the wrong scale, and it will read as a toy.

## The reference is named, and it is outside the subject {#scale-has-a-reference}

State what a size is measured against. "Large" is not a scale; "1.8 m, a head
taller than the rows" is. A production fixes one reference — here a standing
figure — and every extent states itself against it, so a subject that changes
size later cannot quietly change what it is next to.

Sources: `principles/authoring/settings.md#measured-extent`.

## Proportion is stated among the parts {#proportion-is-internal}

Where the parts of a subject relate by a rule — a head to a body, a leaf to its
frame, a course to a wall — state the rule rather than the two numbers. A rule
survives a change of scale; a pair of numbers does not, and the next author who
resizes the subject breaks the relation without noticing.

## A derived size is derived once {#derived-once}

A measurement taken from another — an eye height from a body height, a
clearance from a figure — is computed in one place from the value it depends
on. Written twice, the two answers diverge on the first change, and the frame
shows the one nobody edited.

Sources: `principles/source/subjects.md#measured-values-are-fields`.

## A tolerance is stated where sizes accumulate {#tolerance-is-stated}

Where a size is built from repeated parts — rows of a group, courses of a wall,
a tier standing behind another — say what deviation is acceptable and what it
is measured against. Without it, a small per-part error accumulates into a
visible one and nobody can say which part was wrong.

Sources: `principles/common.md#stated-basis`.
