# Light and legibility

Rules a shot answers about being seen. The light used to *judge* geometry is a
different question and belongs to `principles/review/judgment.md`; confusing
the two is how a broken form ships.

## Delivery light serves the frame, and says what it is for {#delivery-light}

The light a shot delivers is an authored choice with a stated job: separate the
subject from the ground, carry the time of day, or hide what the production has
not built. Say which. A key placed because the frame looked dark is a decision
nobody can review.

Sources: `CINEMATOGRAPHY`.

## Contrast is where the eye goes {#contrast-directs}

The most contrasted thing in the frame is what the audience reads first,
whatever the script intended. Where the subject is not that thing, either the
staging or the light is wrong, and the fix belongs to whichever one the scene
says owns it.

Sources: `principles/authoring/settings.md#reads-at-review-distance`.

## The accent is spent on one subject {#accent-is-scarce}

A production fixes a small palette and reserves its saturated accent for the
subject the film follows. Spending it on a second subject spends the only thing
that made the first one findable in one frame, and no amount of light recovers
it.

Sources: `principles/authoring/settings.md#reads-at-review-distance`.

## A shadow carries information or it is noise {#shadow-informs}

Where a shadow is authored, say what it tells: that a figure stands on the
ground, that a mass is above, that two subjects are at different depths. A
shadow that answers none of those costs render time and adds a shape the eye
must discount.
