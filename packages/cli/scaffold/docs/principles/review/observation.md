# Declared observation

Rules a shot answers about what will be looked at. These bind an authored
record, not a person: a shot declares the views, the instants and the
falsifying conditions its judgment will rest on, and that declaration is what
makes the judgment repeatable by somebody who was not there.

## The view set is declared, not chosen at review time {#declared-view-set}

A shot names the frames and passes its judgment needs, and the reviewer
captures exactly those. A reviewer who picks the view picks the flattering one,
and two reviewers of the same shot then judge two different films.

Declare the `mask` or `outline` pass wherever the question is shape. A shot
that declares only `beauty` frames has declared that nobody will look at its
silhouette.

Sources: `SHOT_CONTRACT` on declared frames as a capture obligation;
`principles/craft/form.md#silhouette-test`.

## Sample the interval, not one instant {#declare-the-instants}

A moving claim names the instants it is sampled at — the opening, the declared
review times, the close — rather than resting on whichever frame looks best. A
single hero still cannot distinguish a motion that holds from one that arrives
and drifts.

Sources: `REVIEW_SHOT` on watching the full interval.

## Every criterion names what would falsify it {#falsifying-condition}

An acceptance criterion states its subject, its time or event, the observable
state, and the result that would refute it. A criterion that ends in an
evaluative word judges nothing, and a criterion fitted to the output it already
produces tests nothing.

Sources: `ACCEPTANCE`; `principles/source/shots.md#acceptance-is-authored-with-
the-shot`.

## Evidence names the state it was taken from {#evidence-is-dated}

A frame, an outcome or a measurement names the compiled state it came from. A
judgment quoting evidence taken before the last change is a judgment about a
film that no longer exists, and nothing in the picture says so.

Sources: `principles/review/judgment.md#staleness-is-real`.
