# Judgment conduct

Rules the act of judging answers. Nothing in the graph binds this document, and
that is deliberate: these rules govern how a person or an agent reaches a
verdict, and there is no artifact that could honestly cite them. A citation
would assert that a source file behaves a certain way, which is not what these
say.

Read it before reviewing anything. It is the shortest list this production has
of ways a review can be sincere and still be worthless.

## Measure, evaluate, decide — in that order {#measure-first}

Never assert a cause, a verdict, or a fix before measuring the geometry and
looking at the result. Reasoning from one number, or from an assumption about
what the code probably does, produces confident wrong fixes: a framing solver
once mistook a rig's joint range for its declared key and cropped every actor's
head.

## Ask the structural question before asking for pixels {#numbers-before-frames}

An extent, a placement, or a missing member is a number in an inspection and
only an impression in a frame. Reaching for a render first is how "the roof
field is empty" gets reported about a roof that is full.

## Attribute a change by rendering the pair {#ab-not-guesswork}

When a change is credited with an improvement, render with and without it
rather than reasoning about which edit helped. A comparison that spans two
edits attributes both to one of them.

## Judge geometry under light that reveals the planes {#judgment-light}

A soft even wash makes a broken shape look passable and a good one look dull,
and both readings are lies. Use a directional key, and use flat shading or
normal display when the question is the shape rather than the material.

## Never claim a fix before showing the verified result {#no-claim-before-evidence}

Announcing a fix before the verified artifact exists is the cried-wolf failure,
and it is the one that costs a reader their ability to trust any later report.
Let the verified artifact carry the claim.

## "Less bad" is not "correct", and the ceiling has a name {#less-bad-is-not-correct}

State the remaining flaws yourself, in the same report and the same voice as
the successes. Where the product cannot do the thing, say so and name what
would have to change; a workaround that produces a passable frame conceals a
missing capability, and the concealment is found by whoever depends on it next.

## A verdict is about one state, and states move {#staleness-is-real}

A judgment is about one state of one artifact. When the design, the source, the
generated output or the frames move, the verdict is about something that no
longer exists — re-reading it is reading about a different film.
