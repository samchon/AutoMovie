# Shot Contract

A tracked shot contract says what a shot must accomplish and which source export implements it. It never stores dense keyframes or an action list.

Bind one project-relative TypeScript module and named export. Declare duration, participants, opening and closing named states, camera readability, semantic events, and review frames. Duration must equal an integer production frame count divided by fps; event windows and review times must lie inside it. Formation participants must reference current formation designs.

Of that list, only duration, camera, and review frames are mandatory. Participants, opening and closing states, and events may each be empty, so a held view of a building or a room is a legal shot without inventing an actor to put in it. The camera is what is never optional: `requiredSubjects` must name at least one subject.

`evidence` cites the screenplay scenes this shot covers. It is optional on the record, and at `source` a scene nothing covers is only a warning, but at `review` and `final` a scene that no shot with a passing realization cites is refused, so an uncited shot is work the film cannot account for. A cited scene id the screenplay index does not declare is refused at every scope, and a resident shot contract with no screenplay index at all is refused before any of this.

An optional `storyTime` pins the shot to the production story clock: `originSeconds` is the story second at shot-local zero, and an optional `rate` above zero maps stretched or compressed time. The pin is independent of the edit — it says when the shot happened, never where it is cut — so two shots may share an origin while sitting far apart in the cut, and a shot cut later may carry the smaller origin. A pin requires the production to declare `storyClock`, and it is what lets a `story-sync` acceptance criterion compare this shot's realized events against another shot's. Omit it and the shot asserts nothing about story time.

Every named opening state, closing state, and event needs at least one machine-checkable predicate. Use joint-angle, world-position, or distance predicates with explicit operands, comparison, value, and tolerance. Descriptive prose gives creative meaning but never proves realization.

A joint-angle predicate needs a performed actor, but the spatial operands of a position or distance predicate do not. Either operand may be a compiled scene node, a formation, a named world landmark, or a literal world point; that is the whole vocabulary, and a landmark or a literal point needs nothing staged at all. A door leaf that swung open, a stair landing that sits at its designed height, a pond edge that clears a wall are all statable as a distance from one node to a point without a figure anywhere in the shot. A landmark and a node are checked at different moments: a landmark must already exist in the tracked world design record and is refused at design scope, while a node id is only resolved when the shot realizes, where an absent node fails the predicate rather than the design.

Edge states are a claim about a handoff, not decoration on one shot. Where the edit cuts from a shot playing to its full duration into another starting at zero, no film time passes, so the compiler requires the outgoing shot's realized closing predicates and the incoming shot's realized opening predicates to be the same measured facts, and reports `film-state-handoff-mismatch` when they differ. Two consequences follow. A continuous handoff must state the shared fact on both sides, in the same predicates, rather than each shot describing its own subject. A scene break — new time, new place, nothing carried across — must leave both edges unclaimed, because there is no shared state to assert and inventing one would make the continuity guarantee a fiction. A trimmed boundary cannot use edge states at all and reports `film-state-handoff-unverifiable` instead.

The coding agent exports `defineShot(id, { scene, contract, build })` from `src`. The build may use loops, source-local helpers, explicit design seeds, compiler-provided `runtimeModels` and compact `formationRuntime`, and `context.engine` oracles. It returns actor runtime facts plus a typed script, stage, blocking, performance, and one `eventSamples` entry per declared event; an explicit `enact` may cite a source-computed clip, while ordinary verbs stay thin and host-synthesized. The production host runs stage → block → perform, ROM/artifact gates, continuity sampling, and independent contract realization. Optional bounded `formationMotions`, `formationSlotMotions`, and `effectCues` remain compact production inputs; `formationSlotMotions` is the sparse channel for what happens to named members of a crowd and is capped by exception count, not crowd size, and one shot may activate at most 128 effect cues, non-overlapping within each zone. Do not return a finished scene or shot, anonymous formation nodes, particle arrays, arbitrary effect programs, per-member curves, or a compliance witness: the compiler owns and measures those facts.

The optional returns below are source-owned by design, and they are how a shot stages what no bounded recipe describes. `models` carries complete generated model records whose meshes your own code constructed; `props` carries semantic prop specifications with their bodies, affordances, articulation, and placement relations; `builtEnvironments` carries structured buildings that stay structured in the compiled artifact, so visible placements and support space are staged from the one record rather than transcribed into a second design. `designReferences`, `designEvidence`, and `designLineages` carry the documents a building was read from, the citations back to them, and the phase, variant, and change lineage over identities the design already published.

The deterministic domains arrive the same way, each beside the binding that makes it a building's own: `fluidDomains` with `waterFeatures`, `softBodyDomains` with `softFurnishings`, `plantingDomains` with `plantingClusters` and `plantingInstallations`, and `serviceNetworks`, which carries its wet zones with it. A binding naming a building this shot does not stage is refused rather than left unchecked, because each fold's validator is handed one building and can only answer about that one; a domain no binding cites is still validated on its own terms, since an unsound recipe nothing cites yet is the one about to be cited. Imported assets are on none of these lists: an external glTF, GLB, or VRM stays a compiler-owned production input registered in the manifest, never sandbox output. Read `GEOMETRY` for the mesh constructors, `WORLD_BUILDING` for the building graph, the domains, and prop placement, and `SOURCE_COMPOSITION` for how a subject class emits them.

IDs, duration, camera, motion references, model wiring, ROM, predicate outcomes, and temporal rules are engine-validated. The compiler samples opening predicates at zero, closing predicates at shot duration, event predicates at declared samples, and required camera subjects at review times. A failed measurement blocks compilation even when source prose claims success.

A required subject is a compiled scene-node id or a formation design id. There is no separate actor namespace, so anything the shot staged as a scene node is nameable: a performed figure, a prop, or a visible building element, which the engine's building lowering emits as a set piece named `<environment>/<element>`. That is how a shot with no cast still declares a subject. A subject the design names is not checked against the scene until the shot realizes, where an unresolved id counts as unread.

Camera validation clips each required subject's extent against the current camera frustum, FOV, clip planes, and production aspect ratio, and a node and a formation are measured on different terms. A node's extent is a vertical segment at its resolved root, running upward from that root by the height its model's rest geometry measures, with the rig standing in when the node draws no model and a default height when the geometry is too small to measure. The segment starts at the root whatever the mesh does, so geometry built above or below its own origin is graded somewhere other than where it draws. It also has no horizontal span, so a long facade is graded as a pole standing at its element origin rather than as its footprint: name the element whose origin sits where you want the frame to hold, or declare several. A formation's extent is the whole unit: its designed bounds under the cue playing at that instant, widened by a member's radius and raised by a member's height, so a crowd is graded as the mass it is rather than as one figure standing at its centroid.

A subject reads when any part of that extent falls inside the frame, so a deliberately cropped shot size still passes: a `close` frame shows roughly the band between 0.71 and 0.99 of the subject's height, which contains neither the subject's feet nor the top of its head. Every shot size is therefore available to a shot that declares its subjects. Validation still does not measure `maxOcclusionRatio`, full-body framing, or pixel visibility. Treat that value as the maximum allowed pixel-occlusion threshold, inspect current beauty, mask, depth, outline, or pose PNGs, and cite the exact required frames through `submitReview`.

Every shot declares at least one review frame so visual review has a reachable exact evidence target. A review frame's time must land on an exact production frame, and it must request at least one pass, without repeating one. Review frames are deliberate evidence points: contact, reveal, formation transition, a continuity boundary, or, in a survey of a built thing, the exact held view you intend somebody to judge.

Each pass a review frame declares is a capture obligation, not a menu. Review is refused with `review-evidence-missing` naming the shot, the frame, its time, and the pass until a current verified PNG exists at exactly that frame index and that pass, and completing the beauty pass discharges none of the others. Request the guide passes needed to judge the criterion; beauty alone cannot prove depth, masking, pose, or outline facts. An acceptance frame criterion may only name a pass its review frame already declares, so adding the criterion and adding the pass are one edit.
