# Formation Design

A tracked formation design record represents a unit, not thousands of caller-authored actor nodes. A production may declare at most 100,000 total members under a 128 KiB estimated compact-runtime budget and an 8 MiB anonymous instance-buffer budget covering matrices and phase attributes. The compiler stores 1,024-slot chunks, not anonymous scene nodes, and refuses larger or more expensive crowds rather than promising unlimited scale.

**The compact-runtime budget is charged per participation, not per unit, and that is what a long production runs out on.** A formation named by six shots is stored six times — its member chunks, its heroes, and a 4,096-byte fixed floor, all multiplied by the number of shots that stage it. Two consequences follow and neither is obvious from the member ceiling. Reusing one unit across a sequence is not free, so a corps that appears in every shot of its sequence is the most expensive thing in the production even if it never grows. And the floor alone bounds the film: thirty-two participations exhaust the whole budget before a single member is counted, so a production plans how many units each shot stages and how many shots each unit appears in, from the first shot rather than at the fortieth. Budget it as `shots x units per shot`, and stage a unit only in the shots that actually show it. A record no shot stages is charged one participation all the same, so a unit you have stopped using is deleted rather than merely dropped from its shot; leaving it registered keeps paying its floor for nothing.

The identity of an ordinary member is `formation id + deterministic slot index`. Count, compact layout, anchor, facing, and seed derive every slot. `line`, `column`, `wedge`, and `perimeter` put positive lateral/depth `spacing` inside the layout because their algorithms consume it. `arc` separation is determined only by radius, covered angle, and count; `scatter` density is determined only by radius, seed, and count. Do not send a common spacing field that those layouts would silently ignore. Line and column capacity must cover count; wedge depth squared must cover count; perimeter ring capacity must cover count; arc/scatter radius must be positive.

`perimeter` is the arrangement a group takes when it faces outward around something: a square, a cordon, a ring of seats, spectators lining an enclosure. It is a rectangular ring around an empty middle, stated as `files` members along each lateral side, `ranks` along each depth side, and `thickness` rings deep; corners belong to the lateral sides, so one ring seats `2 * files + 2 * ranks - 4` and each ring inward is two shorter on every side. Members fill the outer ring from the front-left corner along the front, down the right side, back along the rear and up the left, then continue inward, and a ring short of members is left open at its end rather than redistributed. Reach for it when the middle has to stay empty and the unit has to stay one unit: four separate formations cannot re-form into one another, and a `line` fills its rectangle while an `arc` closes only a circle. A side below two members is refused, because a ring one member wide is a line and should say so; so is a `thickness` that would drive either side below two, because that ring has no inside to place anyone around.

State how tightly the unit is dressed. `line`, `column`, `wedge`, `perimeter`, and `arc` accept an optional `dressing` with a `lateral` and a `depth` tolerance in meters, and each member's deviation is derived from the formation seed and its slot index. Exact geometry reads as one figure repeated on a grid; a real group holds its arrangement to a tolerance, and the width of that tolerance is a story fact, not noise. Omit `dressing`, or leave both numbers at zero, when the shot wants an exact lattice. The deviation costs no storage and regenerates identically, so the same design always compiles to the same group; do not reach for randomness to get the same effect, and do not author per-member offsets. `scatter` takes no `dressing` because its placement is already seeded.

`capabilities` is a small review-facing vocabulary for intended unit behavior. It is not a compiler permission system: the foundation compiler neither infers an action from a label nor proves that source avoided an undeclared action. Source authors choreography in code; typed shot predicates, compiled event realization, and frame review establish what actually happened.

Use `heroOverrides` sparingly when a slot needs a persistent named actor, close camera, unique prop, or individual performance. A hero changes only the node identity: the compiler still enforces the formation's model recipe and base transform before source choreography runs. At most 256 slots of one formation may be promoted, and a design asking for more is refused rather than quietly turned into explicit nodes. Do not promote every member.

Before writing choreography, inspect formation geometry through the programmatic engine API and compare it with world route width. At compile time, inspect `context.formationRuntime[formationId]` for bounded chunks, bounds, hero inventory, LOD, and phase. Regenerate only a needed representative with `context.engine.formationSlot(formationId, index)`; do not recreate layout arithmetic or emit anonymous formation scene nodes. AutoMovie promotes heroes to explicit nodes and rejects collisions.

Source code authors named heroes and optional `formationMotions`: at most 256 non-overlapping cues with one participating formation, action, shot-local interval, easing, and bounded `from`/`to` translation, facing offset, and lateral/depth spacing scale clamped to `0.25..4`. These explicit cues—not `capabilities`—move and deform the anonymous unit. Do not author one arbitrary curve per member. The viewer regenerates exact anonymous transforms, excludes heroes once, samples the compact cues, culls chunks, and selects near/far LOD from distance, representative-member projected contribution, and hysteresis without changing narrative identity.

Anonymous members are not frozen while the unit moves. When a tier's runtime model declares gaits, each is baked once per tier into a table of rigid part transforms and every member replays the playing table at its own seeded phase, so a unit walks rather than sliding across the ground in a shared attitude. Nothing about it is per member: the instance buffers are the size they were, members differ only in where they sit in the cycle, and a tier whose model declares no gait or carries no skeleton renders exactly as it did before. There is no per-member clip to author.

Cadence is the ground the unit covers, not a number you set. One turn of a cycle is one stride, the stride is measured from the baked cycle itself, and the ground is the displacement your cues author, so a unit that covers nothing takes no step and a unit that covers twice as much steps twice as often. A turn about the unit's own origin carries each member over that member's own arc. Author speed by authoring where the unit goes and how long it takes to get there.

A cue may add `gait` to name which of the figure's declared gaits its members perform for the length of that cue, which is how one group holds, then moves, then holds again inside one shot. The name is the recipe's own gait name, and a name no figure of the unit declares is refused rather than silently replaced. Omit it and the cue's `action` label is tried as a gait name before the figure's first declared gait performs. A cycle that carries a body nowhere, such as an idle, runs on its own declared period instead of on the ground.

When something happens to one member and not to its neighbours, author `formationSlotMotions`. Each cue names one participating formation, the exact `slots` it singles out, a shot-local interval, an easing, and `from`/`to` member state: `present`, a unit-local `offset` in meters, and a `facingOffsetDeg`. Retention matches the unit-level channel—identity before the first cue, interpolation inside one, and the cue's `to` state held after it ends—so a member whose `to` is absent stays out of the shot, and one that stepped aside stays aside. `present: false` is removal: the member stops being drawn, stops being measured against the staged space, and stops being counted among the drawn, while the unit's designed count, bounds, and centroid are unchanged. The channel is sparse by contract: at most 256 cues singling out at most 1,024 members in one shot, so its cost is the exceptions and never the crowd. A slot promoted to a named actor is refused here—author that member on its actor node, which already owns a full performance surface.

Compilation refuses a unit standing inside itself or inside another. Each member is measured as the vertical column its own compiled parts fill, on the terrain under it, at cue endpoints and at sampled instants between them, after both cue channels have moved or removed anyone; two members whose columns meet at any measured instant fail the shot, and the diagnostic names the time, the slots, how far apart they stand, and the clearance their bodies need. The column is inscribed in a member rather than drawn around it and time is sampled rather than solved, so the gate can miss an overlap but never invent one. A refusal therefore means two bodies really share a place: widen `spacing`, redress the unit, or correct the cue that closed the gap. There is no tolerance to raise.

A camera frames a unit by naming it in a group target's `formations`, beside any `nodes` framed with it. The solve then measures the unit's real transformed bounds—the footprint the cue has moved and rescaled, widened by a member's radius and raised by a member's height—and stands the camera back far enough to hold that width across the delivery raster as well as that height. Do not list slot ids to frame a crowd: ordinary members are compiler-owned, and a centroid point frames two thousand members exactly as it frames one. A formation is a mass a camera frames, not one body with one point to aim at, so `lookAt`, `reach`, a gesture aim, and a `launch` aim each refuse it; name the staged nodes to aim at instead.

## Re-forming

A unit's `layout` says how it is arranged, once, for the whole production. A cue names `layout` when the unit *changes* arrangement: a line becoming a column, a block falling into an arc, a scatter closing into ranks. Each member travels from its place in the design's arrangement to its place in the named one, in the unit's own frame, on the cue's declared easing — so a re-form and a turn compose instead of one choosing the other's frame. Any two arrangements are *expressible* as a re-form whatever their kinds, because what is blended is where a member stands and not the numbers that put it there.

Expressible is not the same as accepted. A member keeps its slot number across the change and travels straight from one place to the other, so two members whose straight lines cross meet somewhere in the middle, and the overlap gate walks that middle and refuses the shot. The further the two arrangements disagree about who stands where — a line closing into a ring, a column opening into an arc — the more of the unit crosses. There is no assignment step that quietly renumbers members to avoid it, because a slot is an identity a hero override and a per-member cue both name, and silently reassigning it would move the wrong member. Open the unit with `spacingScale` for the length of the transit so the paths clear each other, take the change through an intermediate arrangement in two cues, or give the re-form long enough that the gate's sampled instants are the only thing it has to survive. A refusal names the time and the two slots; read it as a statement about the paths and not about the destination.

`spacingScale` is not this. Opening and closing an arrangement is not changing it; a unit that has to arrive somewhere its spacing cannot reach names a `layout`.

A cue naming a lattice that cannot seat every member is refused at compile, and the ground and overlap gates walk the interior of a re-form: a crowd whose two arrangements both stand on floor, and whose path between them does not, is refused for the moment it is over nothing. Omit `layout` and the unit keeps the arrangement it is in.
