# Formation Design

A tracked formation design record represents a unit, not thousands of caller-authored actor nodes. A production may declare at most 100,000 total members under a 128 KiB estimated compact-runtime budget and an 8 MiB anonymous instance-buffer budget covering matrices and phase attributes. The compiler stores 1,024-slot chunks, not anonymous scene nodes, and refuses larger or more expensive crowds rather than promising unlimited scale.

The identity of an ordinary member is `formation id + deterministic slot index`. Count, compact layout, anchor, facing, and seed derive every slot. `line`, `column`, and `wedge` put positive lateral/depth `spacing` inside the layout because their algorithms consume it. `arc` separation is determined only by radius, covered angle, and count; `scatter` density is determined only by radius, seed, and count. Do not send a common spacing field that those layouts would silently ignore. Line and column capacity must cover count; wedge depth squared must cover count; arc/scatter radius must be positive.

State how tightly the unit is dressed. `line`, `column`, `wedge`, and `arc` accept an optional `dressing` with a `lateral` and a `depth` tolerance in meters, and each member's deviation is derived from the formation seed and its slot index. Exact geometry reads as one figure repeated on a grid; a real group holds its arrangement to a tolerance, and the width of that tolerance is a story fact, not noise. Omit `dressing`, or leave both numbers at zero, when the shot wants an exact lattice. The deviation costs no storage and regenerates identically, so the same design always compiles to the same group; do not reach for randomness to get the same effect, and do not author per-member offsets. `scatter` takes no `dressing` because its placement is already seeded.

`capabilities` is a small review-facing vocabulary for intended unit behavior. It is not a compiler permission system: the foundation compiler neither infers an action from a label nor proves that source avoided an undeclared action. Source authors choreography in code; typed shot predicates, compiled event realization, and frame review establish what actually happened.

Use `heroOverrides` sparingly when a slot needs a persistent named actor, close camera, unique prop, or individual performance. A hero changes only the node identity: the compiler still enforces the formation's model recipe and base transform before source choreography runs. Do not promote every member.

Before writing choreography, inspect formation geometry through the programmatic engine API and compare it with world route width. At compile time, inspect `context.formationRuntime[formationId]` for bounded chunks, bounds, hero inventory, LOD, and phase. Regenerate only a needed representative with `context.engine.formationSlot(formationId, index)`; do not recreate layout arithmetic or emit anonymous formation scene nodes. AutoMovie promotes heroes to explicit nodes and rejects collisions.

Source code authors named heroes and optional `formationMotions`: at most 256 non-overlapping cues with one participating formation, action, shot-local interval, easing, and bounded `from`/`to` translation, facing offset, and lateral/depth spacing scale. These explicit cues—not `capabilities`—move and deform the anonymous unit. Do not author one arbitrary curve per member. The viewer regenerates exact anonymous transforms, excludes heroes once, samples the compact cues, culls chunks, and selects near/far LOD from distance, representative-member projected contribution, and hysteresis without changing narrative identity.

When something happens to one member and not to its neighbours, author `formationSlotMotions`. Each cue names one participating formation, the exact `slots` it singles out, a shot-local interval, an easing, and `from`/`to` member state: `present`, a unit-local `offset` in meters, and a `facingOffsetDeg`. Retention matches the unit-level channel—identity before the first cue, interpolation inside one, and the cue's `to` state held after it ends—so a member whose `to` is absent stays out of the shot, and one that stepped aside stays aside. `present: false` is removal: the member stops being drawn, stops being measured against the staged space, and stops being counted among the drawn, while the unit's designed count, bounds, and centroid are unchanged. The channel is sparse by contract: at most 256 cues singling out at most 1,024 members in one shot, so its cost is the exceptions and never the crowd. A slot promoted to a named actor is refused here—author that member on its actor node, which already owns a full performance surface.

A camera frames a unit by naming it in a group target's `formations`, beside any `nodes` framed with it. The solve then measures the unit's real transformed bounds—the footprint the cue has moved and rescaled, widened by a member's radius and raised by a member's height—and stands the camera back far enough to hold that width across the delivery raster as well as that height. Do not list slot ids to frame a crowd: ordinary members are compiler-owned, and a centroid point frames two thousand members exactly as it frames one. A formation is a mass a camera frames, not one body with one point to aim at, so `lookAt`, `reach`, a gesture aim, and a `launch` aim each refuse it; name the staged nodes to aim at instead.
