# Battle Simulation Handbook

Battle scenes combine historical evidence, object capability, repeated formations, engine facts, and authored human response. Separate those sources so confidence and creativity remain honest.

## Three evidence tiers

1. **Fact**: deterministic engine results such as position, range test, hit time, impact vector, event order, collision, and registered weapon statistics. Do not rewrite these because a different result would look better.
2. **Hint**: a physically or tactically plausible default derived from facts, such as recoil direction, a balance correction, smoke drift, spacing response, or likely suppression. It is useful but not mandatory performance.
3. **Authored response**: expression, hesitation, discipline, panic, recovery, attention, and stylized emphasis. Accept the hint, transform it, or deliberately depart from it while preserving the immutable facts and declaring the reason.

This separation prevents two failures: treating a simulation hint as historical destiny, and letting dramatic invention contradict the shot’s own measured event.

## Research discipline

Use `docs/research/object-standards.md` for object coordinate, rig, import, and capability evidence. Use `docs/research/napoleonic-line-battle.md` for the line-battle evidence ledger. Keep source confidence attached to claims:

- primary or official standards constrain implementation;
- scholarly secondary evidence supports doctrine, equipment, and organization;
- museum or specialist references support material and handling detail;
- uncertain visual convention remains explicitly interpretive.

Never freeze a disputed drill interval, range, casualty rate, or formation count as universal truth. State the scenario, unit, period, source, and uncertainty.

## Formation authorship

Define doctrine before slots: line, column, square, skirmish order, battery, reserve, or another bounded arrangement. Specify rank/file spacing, facing, anchor, hero exceptions, unit count, weapon capability, and command relationships. Let compact formation recipes materialize repeated units deterministically.

Preserve alignment and intervals until an authored cause changes them. Turning, advancing, wheeling, opening files, recoiling, or breaking formation changes more than centroid: facing, local slots, cadence, contacts, and camera-readable silhouette must evolve together.

## Event ladder

Author semantic events for orders, aim, discharge, projectile or abstracted shot, impact, reload phases, movement, and morale response. Engine-derived event time is authoritative for impact sound and reaction onset. Give smoke, flash, recoil, sound, and visible response their own onset, envelope, region, and source; do not collapse them into one instantaneous effect.

For volleys, small deterministic variation may preserve individuality, but the command and collective action remain legible. A battle is not improved by random noise that destroys formation identity.

## Human and object performance

Weapons need registered grips, muzzle or emission point, stance requirements, moving parts, reload affordances, and statistics. Verify support-hand reach and line of fire. Keep muzzle direction, projectile or hit vector, recoil, smoke, and sound spatially coherent.

Human responses depend on training, fatigue, visibility, command, proximity, prior losses, and story state. Author glances, cadence changes, spacing corrections, flinch, freeze, aid, refusal, or resolve as observable behavior. Avoid making every unit perform the same emotional loop.

## Camera and edit

Use wide views for doctrine and spatial relation, medium views for coordinated action, and close views for choice and consequence. Preserve screen direction and force vectors across cuts unless the film deliberately disorients. Do not let smoke or crowd density hide the exact event the beat and acceptance contract require.

## Verification

Check formation counts and bounds, unit overlap, terrain fit, weapon reach and direction, event order, impact location, reaction latency, sound sync, smoke bounds, camera readability, and continuity of survivors, objects, and state. Review historical plausibility separately from dramatic effectiveness; both should be explicit.
