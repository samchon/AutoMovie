# Screenplay Writing Handbook

This handbook owns prose craft for a production whose selected kind is `film`. `EVIDENCE_GRAPH` owns production-kind selection, folders, stages, and citations. A `brief` has one bounded delivery hierarchy, and a `library` may have no timed prose, so neither inherits the film ladder by default.

A film refines settings through storylines, scenarios, and screenplay before shots, edit, and sound realize it. The prose is one contract branch among production settings and independent design branches, not the root that makes every model, space, motion, or system its subordinate.

## Preserve the ladder

Storylines, scenarios, and screenplay mirror the same ordered files and exact anchored identities: H2 sequence, H3 scene, and H4 beat. One file may contain several addressed units. A downstream unit refines the same identity at the same level without merging, splitting, reordering, or silently replacing it. When added detail exposes a false boundary or hidden unit, repair the earliest storyline owner and propagate the corrected identity.

**Settings** fix the production promise, audience access, world and subject facts, capabilities, constraints, units, and shared audiovisual grammar that later prose actually uses. Make a specific bounded choice when the request delegates one. Record a genuine absence or unresolved decision honestly instead of disguising it as a measured fact.

**Storylines** are detailed narrative treatments. Each unit develops an event, relationship, process, observation, or formal operation from an inherited condition into an audience-facing change or accumulation. Causal, relational, observational, procedural, non-human, episodic, and other formal development are all valid when the selected form gives the unit a function, connection, and identifiable result. Where agency operates, write the consequential choices, available alternatives or resistance, and reactions that shape it. Do not invent conflict or require one governing question for a form that does not use them. Leave executable staging, final wording, exact timing, camera, and implementation downstream.

**Scenarios** refine each same-identity unit into a production-capable initial script. Establish the entry state, participants or active forces, observation range, objective or organizing process, applicable obstruction, ordered action and reaction, spatial or informational movement, consequential exchange, knowledge change, and exit state. Write physical or formal progression deeply enough that the screenplay does not invent essential scene mechanics. A scene may contain several actions and exchanges, but every one must obey the settings and remain traceable to the inherited progression.

**Screenplay** is the final human-readable audiovisual requirement. It preserves decisive scenario action and boundary states, then chooses what the audience sees, reads, hears, and intentionally does not hear, with the dialogue, captions, sound, silence, perceptual access, rhythm, implication, and render-critical time needed for independent realization. It does not predetermine how many shots realize a scene or which story moments one shot may contain.

The typed screenplay index gives prose units stable identifiers, paths, exact beat coverage, continuity joins, and downstream references. It is a semantic join into the full treatment and screenplay, never a second abbreviated screenplay. Stable identities survive wording changes; topology changes require an explicit upstream revision and propagated references.

## Write audiovisual blocks

Begin each H4 with audience-facing screenplay blocks rather than a treatment summary. Put visible action, spoken or written words, sound, and meaningful silence in their actual order, and split a block when its subject, action, speaker, audible layer, or presentation state changes materially.

- A conventional scene heading may state INT., EXT., or INT./EXT., a named location, and a time condition when that helps a human production handoff. Typography is a craft convention, not a compiler invariant, and it does not replace the anchored H3 identity.
- Write action in the present tense and make it observable. Translate internal state into behavior, speech, text, sound, silence, or another audience-perceptible consequence when the film must communicate it.
- Give dialogue its final wording when wording, interruption, withholding, register, or rhythm changes the scene. Use O.S. or V.O. when the audible source and audience access require that distinction. Parentheticals carry only a necessary playable adjustment.
- Author silence and pauses when they change information, pressure, rhythm, or audience access. State an exact duration, boundary, or explicit shot-owned timing reference whenever deterministic rendering needs one; a vague pacing adverb is not a clock.
- Preserve production intent that the audience contract depends on, such as a withheld reveal, off-screen source, continuous passage, or exact image-sound relation. Leave lens, camera path, final blocking, shot count, source range, transition implementation, sound-synthesis parameters, geometry, interpolation, and renderer calls to their downstream owners.

## Shape each scene for its form

A scene needs an identifiable local progression, not a universal recipe. A character-driven scene may organize question or want, resistance, turn, and exit value. An observational, procedural, non-human, or formal scene may instead develop information, atmosphere, rhythm, setup, repetition, comparison, or cumulative form. Use only the relations the work needs, and make the resulting audience change or formal effect legible.

Enter late or leave early when compression preserves the setup, action, and consequence the audience needs. Establish more when orientation or aftermath is the scene's function. Contrast, causality, setup and payoff, reversals, and dramatic questions are available craft structures, not mandatory tests every film or scene must pass.

Externalize required state through production-specific choices. Distance, breath, gaze, an interrupted reach, spacing drift, cadence, a prop, a landmark, a sound change, or a refusal can make an otherwise internal change perceptible. Use a detail because it changes action, relation, atmosphere, emotion, theme, or audience understanding, not because a screenplay category looks empty.

## Test realization without taking it over

For every H4 beat, ask:

1. What visible, written, audible, or intentionally silent state reaches the audience at entry?
2. What action, exchange, process, observation, or formal development changes it?
3. Which registered subjects, active forces, or audiovisual sources carry that change?
4. Which semantic event and acceptance condition could falsify its realization?
5. Can downstream shot, edit, and sound owners realize it without inventing narrative or audience content?
6. Does every render-critical duration have a number or an explicit timing owner?

If the answer depends on an unavailable asset, impossible articulation, invisible required state, or unbounded simulation, repair the earliest owner or design the missing capability deliberately. Diffusion may repaint a reviewed structure; it does not invent the semantic structure.

Realization proceeds progressively from reviewed prose and its typed index into shot contracts and source, compiled shots, film edit and sound mapping, and whole-film viewer playback. In this repository those surfaces serve the practical functions of storyboard, animatic, layout, and previs by making composition, staging, motion, timing, continuity, and rhythm inspectable before a finished look. They do not certify live-production cost, safety, legal clearance, vendor delivery, or a photoreal finished shot.

## Revise from the owner

Read the screenplay as a viewer and aloud. Remove repeated information, generic intensifiers, unfilmable explanation, and implementation choices that a downstream owner can solve equivalently. Verify stable identity, inherited boundary state, spatial and knowledge continuity, audience access, proportional timing, and the work-specific relation that connects each unit.

When a screenplay or compiled realization reveals a defect, correct the earliest owner that made the decision. Propagate the consequence through each affected downstream scenario, screenplay, typed-index, shot-source, film-source, compilation, evidence, and review owner. Soft-lock the screenplay only when the ladder is coherent; a justified revision makes its downstream dependents stale and rebuilds them instead of allowing silent drift.
